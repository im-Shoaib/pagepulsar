Why Isn't My Business Showing Up on Google? 9 Reasons and Fixes

Missing from Google Maps, the normal search results, or both? Here's a three-search test to find the problem, and a fix for each cause.

You search for your own business on Google and it isn't there. Or a competitor two streets away shows up and you don't. Or your website appears, but you're nowhere on the map.

I run into this a lot when I look at small business websites. The cause is nearly always one of a few simple things, and most of them take minutes to check. Some take a few days to fix, because Google has to catch up after you change something. Here's how to work out which problem you have, and what to do about each one.

First, work out which part of Google you're missing from

Google shows local businesses in two separate places, and each one runs on different information.

  • The map results. That's the box with a small map and three businesses under it, plus Google Maps itself. These come from your Google Business Profile.
  • The normal results. The list of website links underneath. These come from your website.

You can easily show up in one and not the other. So before you change anything, open a private or incognito window (so your own search history doesn't get in the way) and try these three searches:

  1. Your exact business name and town. If your business panel with photos, hours and reviews doesn't appear, the problem is your Business Profile.
  2. site:yourdomain.com, using your own web address. If Google finds nothing, your website hasn't been indexed at all.
  3. What a customer would actually type, like "roofer in Dallas" or "dentist near me". If you appear for your name but not here, Google knows you exist. It's just putting other businesses above you.

Now find your situation below. Reasons 1 to 6 are about the map. Reasons 7 to 9 are about your website.

Why you're not showing on Google Maps

1. You don't have a Business Profile, or you can't get into it

If you never set one up, there may be no listing at all. Or Google may have made a basic one from public information that nobody has claimed.

Then there's the old-agency problem. A web designer or a former employee set up the profile years ago with their own email address, and now nobody at the business can log in.

Go to Google Business Profile and search for your business name. If it's there, request ownership. If it isn't, create it. You can use the same page to ask for access when someone else owns the listing.

2. Your profile isn't verified yet

Google wants proof that you really run the business. It chooses the verification method for you, and you can't swap it for another one. Depending on your business, it might be a phone call, a text, an email, a video call or a postcard in the mail.

Once you've done your part, Google says its review can take up to five business days, and now and then longer.

Sign in and look at your profile. If there's still a "Verify" or "Get verified" button, you're not finished.

3. Your category or business name is working against you

Your primary category is how Google decides what you are. A dental practice filed under something vague like "Health consultant" won't come up when someone searches for a dentist.

The name is the other trap. Some owners add keywords to it, so "Smith Plumbing" turns into "Smith Plumbing – Emergency Plumber Dallas 24/7". It feels like it should help. But Google's guidelines say the name has to match the real name on your sign, and that adding extra words can get the profile suspended.

Pick the most specific primary category for your main business. Add only the few extra categories you really need. Keep the name exactly as customers see it on your storefront, your van or your invoices.

4. You go to your customers, and your address is set up wrong

Plumbers, cleaners, mobile mechanics and other trades often put their home address on the profile. Google doesn't want that.

If customers don't come to you, Google asks you to remove the address and set a service area instead. You can add up to 20 areas, by city or by ZIP code or postcode. Google also says the whole area shouldn't usually stretch more than about two hours' drive from where you're based.

5. Your profile was suspended

If your listing was there last month and has now vanished, sign in and look for a suspension notice. The usual causes are a keyword-stuffed name, a PO box or virtual office used as the address, or more than one profile for the same business.

Fix whatever broke the rules first, then appeal through the Business Profile help pages. Don't just make a new profile to replace the old one. That tends to make things worse.

6. Other businesses are ranking above you

This is often the real answer, and the least satisfying one. You are on Google. You're just not near the top.

Google says local results depend mainly on three things:

  • Relevance: how well your profile matches what the person searched for.
  • Distance: how far you are from them.
  • Prominence: how well known your business is. Reviews count here, and so do links to your website from other sites.

You can't do much about distance. Someone searching from the far side of town will mostly see businesses near them. Relevance and prominence are where you have room to move. Fill in every section of your profile: services, hours, holiday hours, photos and a proper description. Ask happy customers to leave a review, and reply to the reviews you get, including the bad ones.

Google is also clear that you can't pay for a better map ranking. Ads are a separate product, and they're labeled as ads.

Why your website isn't in the search results

7. Google hasn't found your site yet

A new website doesn't show up the moment it goes live. Google's own documentation says crawling can take anywhere from a few days to a few weeks.

You can give it a nudge. Add your site to Google Search Console (it's free), submit your sitemap, then paste your homepage address into the URL Inspection tool and click "Request indexing". Do it once. Asking over and over won't make it faster.

8. Your site is telling Google to stay away

This one catches a lot of people out, and it's usually a single setting.

  • WordPress: under Settings → Reading there's a box called "Discourage search engines from indexing this site". Developers often tick it while they build the site and forget to untick it at launch.
  • A noindex tag in the page code, which tells Google not to list that page.
  • A robots.txt file that blocks the whole site.

If you use WordPress, check that box first. After that, the URL Inspection tool in Search Console will tell you whether a page is blocked, and by what.

9. Your pages don't say what you do or where you do it

Google mostly reads text. If your homepage is a big photo, a slogan and a heading that says "Welcome to our website", Google doesn't have much to work with. Neither do your customers.

Check these four things:

  • Your page title (the clickable blue line in Google) says what you do and where. Something like "Emergency Plumber in Dallas | Smith Plumbing".
  • Your main heading on the page says the same kind of thing.
  • Your town or service area is written in normal text, not only inside a picture or a logo.
  • Your phone number and address are real text as well, so Google can read them and people on phones can tap to call.

Things that don't work

Some shortcuts people try. They cost time or money and get you nothing:

  • Paying Google to rank higher on the map. There's no such option.
  • Buying reviews. It breaks Google's rules. It's also illegal now in the US, under an FTC rule from 2024, and in the UK since April 2025.
  • A profile for every town you serve when you don't have an office there. This is a common way to get suspended.
  • A long list of town names at the bottom of every page. It doesn't help, and it looks spammy to people and to Google.

How long until you show up?

If the problem was a setting, like the WordPress box or an unfinished verification, you'll usually see a change within a few days to a couple of weeks of fixing it.

Moving up for searches like "plumber near me" takes longer. Think months, not days. It depends on your reviews, your website and how established you are next to the other businesses in your area.

Quick answers to common questions

Why can I see my business on my phone but not on my laptop?

Google adjusts results based on where you are and what you've searched before. Your phone may be using your exact location, while your laptop uses a rougher guess. That's why the incognito test above is worth doing. It gives you a cleaner idea of what a new customer sees.

My business disappeared from Google Maps overnight. Why?

It's usually one of three things: a suspension (look for a notice when you sign in), a recent edit to your profile that Google is reviewing, or a competitor moving above you in the results.

Do I need a website if I already have a Business Profile?

You can show on the map without one. A website still helps, though. It gives Google more to understand about your business, it lets you appear in the normal results as well, and it gives customers somewhere to check you out before they pick up the phone.

Your checklist

  • Search your business name, site:yourdomain.com, and your main service plus your town.
  • Claim and verify your Google Business Profile.
  • Use a specific primary category and your real business name.
  • Remove your address if customers don't visit you, and set your service areas.
  • Add your site to Search Console and submit your sitemap.
  • Make sure nothing on your site says "noindex".
  • Put what you do and where in your page title, main heading and page text.
  • Ask for reviews, and reply to them.
